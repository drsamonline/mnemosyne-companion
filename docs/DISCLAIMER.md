## A study aid, not medical advice

Mnemosyne Companion is a **study aid and memory tool**. It is intended for qualified homoeopathic practitioners, students of homoeopathy and readers of *Mnemosyne Materia Medica*.

It does **not** diagnose, treat, cure or prescribe. It does **not** provide medical advice, and nothing in it should be used to delay, replace or avoid conventional diagnosis, treatment or emergency care.

## Homoeopathy is complementary, not alternative

Homoeopathy is best practised **alongside**, not instead of, appropriate conventional medical care. The evidence base for homoeopathy remains contested, and the material in this app is presented as classical and observational rather than as established modern evidence. Practise accordingly.

## Emergency situations

**In any acute emergency, seek conventional emergency care at once.** Do not rely on a remedy, or on this app, in a crisis.

Emergency signs include, but are not limited to:

- a seizure lasting more than five minutes;
- difficulty breathing or severe breathlessness;
- loss of consciousness, or a baby who is floppy or unresponsive;
- severe dehydration from vomiting or diarrhoea;
- suspected pneumonia;
- heavy bleeding;
- chest pain, or signs of a stroke;
- any rapid deterioration you cannot explain.

**In India, call 108** (or your local emergency number) or go to the nearest hospital immediately. A homoeopathic remedy may be given alongside such care — never in its place.

## Mental health and risk of harm

Where a case involves thoughts of suicide, a wish to die, self-harm, or any risk of harm to the person or to others, **this must never be managed by the prescriber alone**.

Encourage the person to make contact with professional support, or with a trusted clinician or emergency service, at once. Homoeopathic treatment continues alongside that support, not instead of it.

**In India, free 24×7 support is available from Tele-MANAS:**

- **14416**
- **1-800-891-4416**

If someone is in immediate danger, contact emergency services or take them to the nearest hospital.
